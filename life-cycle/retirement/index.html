Retirement: what to review in your estate plan

Retirement turns a lifetime of saving into a source of income and an inheritance. The rules for retirement accounts matter, because most non-spouse heirs must now empty an inherited account within ten years, with tax consequences.

Schedule a ConsultationAdd to your review list

Worth reviewing

  • Beneficiary designations on every retirement account, and whether a trust should be named
  • Powers of attorney, since you may rely on others more as you age
  • Long-term care planning and how you would pay for it
  • Downsizing, moving, and how any new property is titled
  • Charitable giving and legacy wishes

Documents to look at first

For this life event, these are the documents most often affected. Your own list depends on your family and what you have already signed.

  • Beneficiary designations (retirement, insurance, bank accounts)
  • Revocable living trust
  • Financial power of attorney
  • Health care power of attorney and living will
  • Your will
  • How your home and accounts are titled

When to act

In the year before and after.
