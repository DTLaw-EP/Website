Your Child Turning 18: what to review in your estate plan

At 18, a young person becomes a legal adult. Parents lose automatic authority to speak with doctors, see medical records, or handle money, even if the child is away at school or has a disability.

Schedule a ConsultationAdd to your review list

Worth reviewing

  • A health care power of attorney and records release for your young adult
  • A financial power of attorney
  • For a child with a disability: whether guardianship, or a less restrictive alternative, is the right fit
  • Whether public benefits apply, and how an inheritance should be left to protect them

Documents to look at first

For this life event, these are the documents most often affected. Your own list depends on your family and what you have already signed.

  • Health care power of attorney and living will
  • Financial power of attorney
  • Guardian nominations for children
  • Special needs planning

When to act

Before the 18th birthday.
