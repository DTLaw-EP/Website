Married or moving in together? What to update in your estate plan

Getting married does not cancel an Illinois will, and it does not update your beneficiary forms. Illinois law gives a spouse rights that an unmarried partner does not have, including inheriting without a will and a place near the top of the medical decision-maker list. Start with beneficiary forms, then your will and powers of attorney.

What the law assumes for a spouse, and for a partner

Illinois law gives a spouse a set of rights automatically. An unmarried partner gets none of them unless you create them on paper. Living together does not close the gap, however many years it lasts.

Common law marriage is not valid in Illinois. Couples who live together for years are still treated as unmarried. 750 ILCS 5/214

A civil union is different. Partners in a civil union have the same legal obligations, protections, and benefits as spouses. 750 ILCS 75/20

Who decides your medical care if you cannot?

This is the question couples overlook most. If you have signed a health care power of attorney, the person you named decides. If you have not, Illinois falls back on an order set by law for major decisions, such as whether to stop life-sustaining treatment. Here it is, in order.

Paperwork beats the will for many accounts

Retirement accounts and life insurance go to whoever is named on the form, not to whoever your will names. That is why beneficiary forms come first after a wedding or a move-in.

Thinking about a premarital agreement?

A premarital agreement is a contract between two people who are about to marry. It takes effect when they marry, so it has to be made first. It must be in writing and signed by both of you. 750 ILCS 10/2, 10/3

Illinois lets a couple cover several things in one, including:

  • who owns and controls property, including property each person brings to the marriage;
  • what happens to property at separation, divorce, or death;
  • changing or giving up spousal support;
  • a will or trust that carries out the agreement; and
  • who receives life insurance proceeds.

It cannot reduce a child’s right to support. 750 ILCS 10/4 Not every couple needs one, but the decision is worth making on purpose. Unmarried couples are not covered by this law, so an agreement between them about shared property is worth discussing with an attorney.

Common questions

Does getting married cancel my Illinois will?

No. Marriage does not revoke a will. An older will keeps working as written, even if it names someone you no longer want or does not mention your new spouse. Divorce is different: it cancels gifts and appointments to a former spouse in a will signed before the divorce. 755 ILCS 5/4-7

Is my partner my heir if we have lived together for years?

No. Illinois does not recognize common law marriage, so your partner has no right to inherit unless you leave property to them in a will or trust, or by a beneficiary form or joint title. A civil union is different, because civil union partners have the same rights as spouses. 750 ILCS 5/214, 75/20

Can my spouse claim more than my will leaves them?

Yes. A surviving spouse can renounce the will and take one-third of the estate if you leave children or other descendants, or one-half if you do not. The spouse must file a written renunciation within 7 months after the will is admitted to probate, unless the court allows more time. 755 ILCS 5/2-8

Who makes medical decisions for me if I cannot and have no document?

Illinois sets an order for major decisions: a court-appointed guardian, then your spouse, adult children, parents, siblings, and adult grandchildren, and then a close friend. A partner who is not a spouse can qualify only as a close friend, by giving the doctor an affidavit. A health care power of attorney avoids the question by naming your choice directly. 755 ILCS 40/25

Do we need a premarital agreement?

Not every couple does. It makes the most sense when one person brings significant assets, a business, children from an earlier relationship, or expects an inheritance. It must be in writing and signed by both people before the marriage, and it cannot reduce a child’s right to support. Each person is usually better served by having their own lawyer. 750 ILCS 10/3, 10/4

Should we put each other on the deed to our home?

It depends. Married couples can hold a home as tenants by the entirety, so the survivor keeps the whole property, but the deed has to say so. Joint ownership also has tradeoffs, including losing sole control and exposing your share to the other owner’s situation. Talk it through before you retitle. 765 ILCS 1005/1c
