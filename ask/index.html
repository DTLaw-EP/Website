Questions we have answered

General information about Illinois law, not legal advice. Each answer is a general explanation and may not apply to your situation.

What if my child has a disability?

Leaving money directly to a person who receives needs-based public benefits can affect eligibility. Special needs trusts exist for this reason, and the rules are detailed, so please speak with an attorney about your own situation. Read our special needs trust basics

What does probate mean?

Probate is the court process that confirms a will, pays debts and distributes property. Smaller estates may qualify for a simpler process using a small estate affidavit. In Illinois, the limit is currently $150,000. What happens without a will

How often should I review my plan?

Every few years, and after any major life event or change in the law, such as a marriage, divorce, birth, move, or a big change in your assets. Beneficiary forms in particular are worth checking after each of these.

Do I need a trust?

Many families do not. A trust can help when children are young, when you want to control the timing of an inheritance, or when avoiding probate matters. Whether one fits depends on your family and your assets. Read our free guide

Is a will enough?

A will covers property in your name alone and lets you name a guardian for minor children. It does not control accounts that have beneficiary forms, or jointly owned property. Many families use a will together with beneficiary forms and powers of attorney.

Does naming a guardian override the other parent?

No. If one parent survives, that parent generally continues to care for the child. A named guardian matters most when neither parent can. A written choice tells the court who you trust, and the court still decides what is best for the child. Read our new-baby guide
